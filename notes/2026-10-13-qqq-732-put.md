## Why the book sells flush lows and buys bounce tops

1. **An EMA20 cross confirms a move after most of it is done.** On 10-12 the EMA20 was 1.97 above the 747.10 low when the call crossed it, 73% of the way back up the 2.69-point flush. The bounce topped at 87%, 0.02 over the call's close. On 10-13 the EMA20 was flat at 740.00–740.20 from 08:20 to 08:31, while each of the first two bars spanned about a point, so either side could close across it on any bar.
2. **The one bullet goes at the open.** Replaying the book as it ran today over the ten sessions in the data (09-30 through 10-13), the first legal entry came by 08:35 on 8 of 10 days (7 of 10 counting fresh crosses only).
3. **Every filter looks at one bar.** RSI14 can swing 20 points or more in one bar at the open: 24.9, 50, 34.6, 28.9, and 50 at 08:29–08:33 today, and 17.8 to 44.1 on 10-12 (08:34 to 08:35). One opening bar resets it, so the 30/70 skip passes the bar after a flush. VWAP is the opening print for the first minutes. Nothing asks how far price has already run, where the bar sits in the swing, or whether the side just flipped.
4. **So the first signal lands on the wrong side.** In the replay, the first legal entry reached +0.6 QQQ in its favor before −1.5 against on 3 of 10 days, or 5 of 10 on fresh crosses only. Those distances are roughly the +20% latch and the −50% stop on these wings. A first-hour bar picked at random, on either side, wins that race 75% of the time (70% for bars from 08:30 to 08:35). The open signal does worse than picking a side at random. 10-12 and 10-13 are the two live examples: the call at the top of the bounce, then the put at the bottom of the flush.

## Pointers

1. Don't trade a fresh flip. If the signal pointed the other way in the last five minutes, premarket included, skip it. Three minutes is not enough: Monday's call came five minutes after the last put signal.
2. Or wait one bar. If the next minute doesn't agree, or it breaks a rule, skip the trade. Either rule skips both of the last two losers. Using both barely changed the result.
3. Fix the first-hour filter's starting point. It uses this morning's first price as its regular-hours average, so it would not have stopped Monday's call.
4. After a gap, don't sell into the gap at the bell. Today opened 6.5 points down, and the put was bought under the premarket low after RSI had already been under 30. The selling had already happened.
5. Test the other three on the bot's own numbers before using them. The RSI block, the VWAP distance, and the capitulation filter each turn on numbers this replay can't match exactly.
